## 2. What the numbers say

### 2.1 Start and replay (Q1)

- **Where the time goes.** The snapshot start is mostly the start of the .NET runtime. AOT on the desktop: app ready after 624 ms out of 936 ms. The empty app at 6x takes 3.0 s (interpreted) and 3.6 s (AOT) before any data is read (T1, T4). Reading, decrypting and parsing the snapshot plus 1,000 tail events adds about 0.3 s on the desktop and about 1.7 s at 6x.
- **Full replay of 100,000 events.** It meets 10 s on the desktop. At 4x and 6x it misses 10 s with both builds: 17 s and 25 s for AOT, 31 s and 45 s interpreted.
  - At 6x AOT the replay splits into IndexedDB read 5.0 s, Web Crypto decryption 6.2 s, unpadding and copying 1.3 s, JSON parsing 6.7 s and projection 0.5 s (T2).
  - Read and decryption are browser work that AOT does not change. Their size follows the stored bytes (see 2.5).
- **Complexity (PF6).** 10 times the data costs 5.9 to 9.6 times the replay time in every configuration (T3), so PF6 holds. AOT comes closest to 10× because its fixed costs are smaller.
- **Storage.** IndexedDB holds 1.65 kB per event (164.6 MB for 100,000 events), measured with the provisional bucket list. Peak WebAssembly memory after the replay is 145 MB in every configuration. The data is safe in every case: the projection checksum matched the reference in all runs.
- **Review trigger.** The engine row's review trigger is reached in the fallback: PF1 and the 10 s target are missed at 4x and 6x. The numbers point at the runtime start and at Web Crypto plus read volume, not at IndexedDB as an engine. A phone run decides whether the trigger really holds (bet card in `docs/adr/bets.md`; not changed here).

### 2.2 AOT against interpreted (Q2)

- **Speed.** AOT is 1.7 to 1.8 times faster on full replay in Chromium (desktop 7.15 → 4.13 s; 6x 44.7 → 25.0 s) and 2.0 times faster in Firefox (11.6 → 5.8 s).
  - The JSON parse runs 3.7 to 5.4 times faster. The snapshot start is 1.4 to 1.6 times faster in Chromium and 1.9 times faster in Firefox.
  - The empty app starts about 10 to 20 % slower with AOT: 539 → 600 ms on the desktop, 3.0 → 3.6 s at 6x.
- **Size.** AOT doubles the first load: 4.36 MB instead of 2.22 MB with Brotli (measured transfer 4.38 against 2.24 MB, 41 files each). Publishing takes 127 s instead of 30 s.
- **Plan rule.** The plan says to choose AOT if it saves "more than about a factor of 2 on full replay for a size growth the owner accepts", and to let the smaller download win "if interpreted is fast enough". Neither case is clear-cut:
  - the factor is just under 2 in Chromium and 2.0 in Firefox;
  - interpreted is not fast enough: it misses PF1 even on the desktop (1.32 s cold).
- The mixed form (AOT only for the replay assembly) is not offered by the .NET 10 WebAssembly tooling as a supported switch; not built.

### 2.3 Durability (Q3)

- **Result.** There were 940 kills or closes over 11 variants in Chromium (the full Playwright browser, `--version` 153; the timing series ran the headless shell, user agent 157) and Firefox 155: SIGKILL while writing, during a snapshot write, during a draft write, and during the open after a kill; plus orderly closes. All 11 variants had 0 findings (T5):
  - no acknowledged event lost;
  - no half-visible batch;
  - every database opened;
  - no snapshot or draft lost.
- **Unacknowledged writes.** Batches written but not yet acknowledged were sometimes found after a restart (allowed by the plan). The harness counted 4 to 21 such batches per variant.
- **`relaxed` against `strict`.** `relaxed` and `strict` in Chromium did not differ in safety. Strict acknowledged about 13 % fewer writes in the same time window (median 83 against 96 per run). That is a rough cost, not a Q1 timing.
- **Not tested.** Process kills cannot show what `relaxed` risks: on an operating-system crash or a power loss, data that is still in the OS cache can be lost. The plan says power loss is not testable here.

### 2.4 Search (Q4, 10,000 items)

- **Query speed.** Once the index is ready, every kind of query answers far within PF3 (200 ms): at worst p95 21 ms at 6x interpreted, 2 ms on the desktop with AOT (T6).
- **When the index is ready.** It matters whether the first search can come right after start. Time from tree visible to the first hits:

  | | A (built in memory) | B (persisted, encrypted) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | AOT, desktop | 0.35 s | 0.29 s |
  | AOT, 6x | 3.2 s | 2.2 s |
  | interpreted, 6x | 11.5 s | 6.3 s |

- **Start time.** Building A does not delay the tree. Tree visible is the same for A, B and C within about 0.3 s.
- **B against C.** B and C cost the same: decrypting 2.7 MB of index chunks is lost in the noise. The privacy cost of C buys no measurable speed (numbers for the open decision "Optional unencrypted search index").
- **Updates.** An incremental update costs 57 to 85 µs per event with AOT on the desktop, and 415 to 425 µs at 6x.

### 2.5 Size limits (Q5)

- **Bucket list.** The provisional list (256 B, 1, 4, 16 KiB) adds 137.6 % overhead, because 96.5 % of events are padded to 1 KiB. Lists with a factor of 2 add 31.5 % (T7); stored bytes would fall by about 45 %, and IndexedDB read and decryption time with them.
- **Long notes** (T8; 1,000 events, 100 notes of the given length). Saving or loading one note takes at most 16 ms (AOT) and 28 ms (interpreted). Memory, however, grows with note length:

  | Note length (characters) | WebAssembly memory | Per note |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 50,000 | 84 MB | – |
  | 200,000 | 230 MB | about 1.8 MB |
  | 1,000,000 | 0.93 to 1.06 GB | about 9 MB |

  At 1,000,000 characters a phone would run out of memory.
- **Pairing code.** With one tree, a 64-character user name and a 100-character server address, the code has 306 bytes and fits QR version 15 at level M. With 10 trees in one code it needs version 40 (T9). The code layout is a spike assumption.

## 4. Not measured, and validity limits

- **Phone.** Q1, Q2 and Q4 on a phone are estimated through CPU throttling. The fallback does not model storage speed, thermal throttling or memory pressure. The plan's memory cap was not applied. Q3 on a phone (`am force-stop`, swipe away) has not run.
- **Firefox and Safari.** The manual kills in the owner's own Firefox have not run. Safari and iOS are not part of the plan.
- **Caching.** The warm HTTP cache stands in for the service worker cache. Web Crypto decryption was slowed by the throttling too (0.7 → 6.2 s).
- **Simplifications.** The replay is a throwaway projection, simpler than the real Domain. The plan's risk row asks to re-run the two key scenarios on the real Domain at step 2a. Kill-test writes carry 64-byte payloads, not full envelopes. Power loss and OS crashes are not testable.
- **Bucket list.** Q1 was measured with the provisional bucket list. With the proposed list the read and decryption phases should shrink; Q1 should be repeated once the list is fixed (plan risk row).

## 5. Conflicts with the plan and the ADRs

- **Dataset size.** The plan estimates 30 to 60 MB; the dataset has 169 MB (IndexedDB 165 MB). The cause is the provisional bucket list, together with the envelope fields and the base64 text. Not changed in the plan.
- **Event mix.** The plan's event mix was corrected to the current format (ADR-082 to ADR-085). Creates are now 10 % instead of 12 %, so that the dataset has exactly 10,000 items. The questions and thresholds are unchanged.
- **Engine review trigger.** The engine row's review trigger is reached in the fallback (2.1). The ADR is not changed; the owner decides after the phone run.
- **No ADR changed.** No ADR and no part of `architecture.md` was changed.
