**Status and evidence.** This report recommends; the owner decides (section 3).
- **Desktop:** Chromium and Firefox, plus CPU throttling 4x and 6x as phone fallback (¹; full rows in T0).
- **Phone and tablet (²):** one phone and one tablet, run by the owner with the on-device runner, without a computer:
  - **Phone:** Fairphone 5, Android 15, 8 GB, so above the plan's 4 to 6 GB class; Firefox 159 and Chrome 140.
  - **Tablet:** Galaxy Tab S6 Lite (SM-P620), 4 GB; Chrome 157 only.
  - **Firefox private tab:** the Firefox phone series ran in a private tab. Only AOT start and replay were repeated in a normal tab (3+3 runs, close to the private-tab numbers, `*-normal-tab.json`). The Firefox interpreted series and Q4 rest on the private-mode store.
  - **Firefox block measurement (T10):** ran in a normal tab.
- **Limits of the device runs:** no adb, so cold runs had no browser restart and include the download over the owner's Wi-Fi; memory only as WebAssembly memory (README).

## 2. What the numbers say

### 2.1 Start and replay (Q1)

- **Start.** On the phone the empty app needs 1.25 to 1.74 s cold before any data is read. Reading the snapshot and 1,000 tail events adds about 0.5 s with AOT and about 1.5 s interpreted (T1). PF1 is missed on both devices with both builds.
- **Full replay of 100,000 events** (T1, T2):
  - **Phone, AOT:** 8.1 s in Firefox (meets 10 s), 15.9 s in Chrome (misses).
  - **Tablet, AOT:** 10.6 s warm, 18.0 s cold.
  - **Cause of the Chrome miss:** Web Crypto decryption takes 7.0 s in Chrome against 1.9 s in Firefox, the IndexedDB read 3.9 s against 2.25 s (Firefox: private tab). JSON parsing is about equal (2.2 to 2.4 s).
- **Decryption in blocks** (T10; phone, both browsers, 5 runs each, plaintext verified).
  - **What was measured:** the same 100,000 events, stored once more in blocks of 1,000. Each block is encrypted with the tree key through Web Crypto, so the key stays a non-extractable handle (ADR-073). Only read and decrypt were measured, not a replay from blocks.
  - **Chrome:** read 4.5 → 0.28 s, decrypt 7.0 → 0.90 s.
  - **Firefox** (normal tab): read 1.49 → 0.65 s, decrypt 1.84 → 0.19 s.
  - **Estimate**, from the parts and not measured as a whole: a replay from blocks would take about 5 to 6 s in both browsers (Chrome 5.5 to 6.1 s, Firefox 4.8 to 5.6 s). The range depends on whether T2 or T10 gives the per-event baseline; they differ because of runs and tab mode.
- **Complexity (PF6).** 10 times the data costs 5.9 to 9.6 times the replay time in every configuration (T3).
- **Storage and memory.** With the provisional bucket list, the browser reports 165 to 169 MB for the site in Chrome and 199 to 534 MB in Firefox. These figures include other test databases; Firefox stores more. The WebAssembly memory size is 145 MB in every T2 row: a reserved heap size, not the usage. The projection checksum matched the reference in every run.
- **Fallback against devices.** For the .NET work the phone sits between the desktop and the 4x fallback. Chrome's decryption on the phone (7.0 s) is slower even than at 6x (6.2 s), and its read (3.9 s) is about as slow as at 4x (3.4 s) (T2).
- **Outliers.** Single disturbed runs (screen off or another app, owner's note) show as one slow run in a series; the medians are unaffected.

### 2.2 AOT against interpreted (Q2)

- **Replay:**
  - **Warm, AOT is faster:** 2.0× in Firefox on the phone, 1.4× in Chrome on the phone, 1.5× on the tablet.
  - **Cold on the tablet:** only 1.09×.
- **Snapshot start:**
  - **Warm, AOT is faster:** 1.3 to 1.8×.
  - **Cold on the tablet, AOT is slower:** 11.0 s against 5.65 s.
- **Empty app.** Warm in Chrome on the phone, AOT is slightly slower (1.32 against 1.10 s). JSON parsing runs 3.1 to 5.4 times faster with AOT.
- **Download** (T4). 4.36 MB against 2.22 MB with Brotli; measured transfer 4.38 against 2.24 MB.
  - **Tablet's connection:** the empty app took 9.2 s cold with AOT against 4.4 s interpreted.
  - **Phone:** 1.48 against 1.25 s in Firefox.
  - **Cache:** that only the first start pays this is an assumption: the spike has no service worker, and its warm runs are reloads from the HTTP cache.
- **Plan rule.** The plan's first clause ("saves more than about a factor of 2 on full replay") is met only in Firefox on the phone. Its second clause ("interpreted is fast enough") fails too: interpreted misses PF1 everywhere and the 10 s replay on both devices.
- **Mixed form.** AOT only for the replay assembly is not a supported switch in the .NET 10 tooling; not built.

### 2.3 Durability (Q3)

- **Desktop.** 900 kills (SIGKILL) and 40 orderly closes over 11 variants in Chromium and Firefox, with 0 findings (T5). Covered: writes of events, snapshots and drafts, and the open after a kill. No acknowledged event was lost, no batch half visible, and every database opened.
  - **Kill counts:** 200 per variant for the mixed workload; 50 for the snapshot, draft and open variants; 20 for closes. The plan asks for at least 200 per browser and variant.
- **Phone.** 10 swipe-aways while writing in Firefox and 10 in Chrome, in a normal tab, with 0 findings.
  - **Recovery** (opening and checking the store) took up to 1.05 s in Firefox and 2.32 s in Chrome, at about 105,000 events.
  - **Number of rounds:** the owner chose 10 per browser instead of the plan's 20. With 0 findings, a loss rate of up to about 30 % per swipe and browser is not excluded (about 15 % over both).
  - **Limits:** the acknowledgement log lives in the same browser (README).
- **`relaxed` against `strict`** in Chromium. No difference in safety; strict acknowledged about 13 % fewer writes in the same time. What `relaxed` risks on an OS crash or power loss is not testable here.

### 2.4 Search (Q4, 10,000 items)

- **Queries.** Once the index is ready, every query answers far within PF3 (200 ms): at most 6.3 ms (p95) on phone and tablet (T6).
- **Tree visible to first hits** on the phone with AOT (T6; Firefox in a private tab):
  - **A** (built in memory): 0.89 to 0.99 s;
  - **B** (persisted, encrypted): 0.75 to 0.81 s;
  - **interpreted:** 3.2 to 3.4 s for A and 1.9 to 2.1 s for B.
- **Start.** Building A does not delay the tree. B and C cost the same, so C's privacy cost buys no measurable speed (open decision "Optional unencrypted search index").

### 2.5 Size limits (Q5)

- **Bucket list.** The provisional list adds 137.6 % overhead (96.5 % of events are padded to 1 KiB); lists with a factor of 2 add 31.5 % (T7).
- **Long notes** (T8; 1,000 events, 100 notes of the given length).
  - **Saving and loading one note:** on the phone (Chrome) at most 23 ms and 9 ms at 50,000 characters. Tree visible came after 5.5 to 9.0 s, including the on-device generation.
  - **WebAssembly memory:** 84 MB at 50,000 characters on the desktop, 145 MB on the phone. At 200,000 characters about 230 MB on the desktop; at 1,000,000 about 1 GB.
  - **Memory per note:** about 0.9 MB at 100,000 characters, interpolated from the desktop, not measured.
  - **200,000 characters on the phone:** the run stopped in both builds. In an agent check on the desktop, not in the result files, the on-device generator needed about 432 MB for this case, so it stays unmeasured.
- **Pairing code.** One tree, a 64-character user name and a 100-character server address give 306 bytes, which fits QR version 15 at level M (T9). The layout is a spike assumption.

## 4. Not measured, and validity limits

- **Devices.** One phone and one tablet, no Safari or iOS. The phone is above the plan's memory class.
  - **Not run:** Q3 and Q5 on the tablet; on the phone, notes of 200,000 characters (the run stopped) and 1,000,000 (left out); the owner's own Firefox kills.
- **Desktop fallback.** It models neither storage speed, thermal throttling nor memory pressure; the plan's memory cap was not applied.
- **Simplifications.** The replay is a throwaway projection, simpler than the real Domain; the plan asks to re-run the key scenarios on the real Domain at step 2a. Kill-test writes carry 64-byte payloads. Power loss and OS crashes are not testable. Q1 used the provisional bucket list; repeat it once the list is fixed.

## 5. Deviations from the plan, and conflicts

- **Dataset size.** The plan estimates 30 to 60 MB; the dataset has 169 MB, mainly because of the provisional bucket list.
- **Event mix.** Corrected to the current format (ADR-082 to ADR-084): creates are 10 % instead of 12 %, for exactly 10,000 items. Questions and thresholds unchanged.
- **Phone runs without adb.** The owner had no computer: no forced cold start and no CPU control (README). The swipe test ran 10 rounds per browser instead of 20.
- **Kill counts.** 20 to 50 kills per variant on the desktop, except 200 for the mixed workload; the plan asks for at least 200.
- **Snapshots after updates.** The recommendation in section 3 changes architecture.md line 573 (and the update scenario in section 8), if the owner takes it.
- **No ADR changed.** No ADR, bet card or part of `architecture.md` was changed.
