## 2. What the numbers say

### 2.1 Start and replay (Q1)

- **Start.** The snapshot start is mostly the start of the .NET runtime. On the phone the empty app needs 1.25 to 1.74 s cold before any data is read; reading the snapshot and 1,000 tail events adds about 0.5 s. So PF1 is missed on every device with both builds (T1).
- **Full replay of 100,000 events** (T2):
  - **Phone, AOT:** 8.1 s in Firefox (meets 10 s) and 15.9 s in Chrome (misses).
  - **Tablet, AOT, warm:** 10.6 s.
  - **The difference is browser work.** In Chrome on the phone, Web Crypto decryption takes 7.0 s (Firefox 1.9 s) and the IndexedDB read 3.9 s (2.25 s). JSON parsing is equal (about 2.2 to 2.4 s).
- **Remedy, measured: decryption in blocks** (T10; phone, both browsers, 5 runs each). The events were stored once more in blocks of 1,000, each encrypted with the tree key through Web Crypto, so the key stays a non-extractable handle (ADR-073, which asks to batch Web Crypto calls).
  - **Chrome:** decrypt 7.0 → 0.90 s, read 4.5 → 0.28 s.
  - **Firefox:** decrypt 1.84 → 0.19 s, read 1.49 → 0.65 s.
  - **Estimated full replay** from the parts, not measured as a whole: about 6 s in Chrome and 5 s in Firefox.
- **Complexity (PF6).** 10 times the data costs 5.9 to 9.6 times the replay time in every configuration (T3), so PF6 holds.
- **Storage.** IndexedDB holds 1.65 kB per event (165 MB for 100,000 events) with the provisional bucket list; WebAssembly memory after the replay is 145 MB. The projection checksum matched the reference in every run.
- **Fallback against devices.** The phone sits between the desktop and the 4x fallback; the 4x and 6x throttling overstated its slowness.
- **Outliers.** Single disturbed runs (screen off or another app, owner's note) show as one slow run in a series; the medians are unaffected.

### 2.2 AOT against interpreted (Q2)

- **Warm, AOT is faster everywhere:** full replay 1.4× (Chrome, where decryption dominates) to 2.0× (Firefox) on the phone and 1.5× on the tablet; snapshot start 1.2 to 1.8×; JSON parsing 3.1 to 5.4×.
- **Cold, the larger download costs:** 4.36 MB against 2.22 MB with Brotli (T4). Over the tablet's connection the empty app took 9.2 s with AOT against 4.4 s interpreted; on the phone the difference was small (1.48 against 1.25 s in Firefox). With the service worker cache only the first start pays it.
- **Plan rule.** The plan chooses AOT if it saves "more than about a factor of 2" for an accepted size growth, and the smaller download "if interpreted is fast enough". Interpreted is not fast enough: on the phone only AOT replays 100,000 events within 10 s, and only in Firefox.
- The mixed form (AOT only for the replay assembly) is not a supported switch in the .NET 10 tooling; not built.

### 2.3 Durability (Q3)

- **Desktop:** 940 kills or closes over 11 variants in Chromium and Firefox (SIGKILL while writing events, snapshots and drafts, during the open after a kill, orderly closes): 0 findings (T5). No acknowledged event lost, no half-visible batch, every database opened.
- **Phone:** 10 swipe-aways from the recent apps while writing in Firefox and 10 in Chrome, normal tab: 0 findings.
  - **Recovery** (open and check the store) took up to 1.05 s in Firefox and 2.32 s in Chrome at about 105,000 events.
  - **Number of rounds:** the owner chose 10 per browser instead of the plan's 20. With 0 findings, a loss rate up to about 30 % per swipe and browser (15 % over both) is not excluded; the desktop kills carry the strong evidence.
  - **Limits:** the acknowledgement log lives in the same browser (README).
- **`relaxed` against `strict`** in Chromium: no difference in safety; strict acknowledged about 13 % fewer writes in the same time. Process kills cannot show what `relaxed` risks on an OS crash or power loss; not testable here.

### 2.4 Search (Q4, 10,000 items)

- **Queries** answer far within PF3 (200 ms) once the index is ready: at most 6 ms (p95) on the phone (T6).
- **Index ready after tree visible** on the phone with AOT: 0.89 to 0.99 s for A (built in memory) and 0.75 to 0.81 s for B (persisted, encrypted); interpreted 3.2 to 3.4 s against 1.9 to 2.1 s.
- **A does not delay the tree.** B and C cost the same, so the privacy cost of C buys no measurable speed (numbers for the open decision "Optional unencrypted search index").

### 2.5 Size limits (Q5)

- **Bucket list.** The provisional list (256 B, 1, 4, 16 KiB) adds 137.6 % overhead, because 96.5 % of events are padded to 1 KiB; lists with a factor of 2 add 31.5 % (T7), and read and decryption time would shrink with the bytes.
- **Long notes** (T8; 1,000 events, 100 notes of the given length):
  - **Saving and loading** one note stays fast: on the phone at most 23 ms and 9 ms at 50,000 characters.
  - **Memory grows with length:** 145 MB at 50,000 characters, about 230 MB at 200,000 and about 1 GB at 1,000,000 on the desktop.
  - **200,000 characters on the phone:** the case stopped in both builds. The on-device generator needs about 432 MB for it, so this case stays unmeasured; it is not proof against the app.
- **Pairing code.** One tree, a 64-character user name and a 100-character server address give 306 bytes, QR version 15 at level M; 10 trees need version 40 (T9; the layout is a spike assumption).

| AOT | **Use AOT** for release 1 | Faster in every warm measurement on phone and tablet (2.2); costs +2.1 MB, paid at the first, uncached start |

| Local event blocks | **Keep events locally also in encrypted blocks of about 1,000** and replay from them (tree key, Web Crypto, rebuildable; per-event envelopes stay for sync). | Snapshot policy | Write a snapshot about every 1,000 new events and when the app goes to the background; keep the previous one until the new one is committed; keep snapshots across app updates unless their format changes | Snapshot start 2.0 to 2.3 s against full replay 8 to 16 s on the phone; 0 snapshot losses in 100 kills |

## 4. Not measured, and validity limits

- **Devices.** One phone and one tablet. Without adb, cold runs had no browser restart, the download came over the owner's Wi-Fi, and memory was measured only as WebAssembly memory.
  - **Not run:** Q3 and Q5 on the tablet.
  - **Long notes on the phone:** 200,000 characters stopped (2.5); 1,000,000 was left out.
  - **Safari and iOS:** not part of the plan.
  - **The owner's own Firefox:** its manual kills have not run.
- **Desktop fallback.** CPU throttling models neither storage speed, thermal throttling nor memory pressure; the plan's memory cap was not applied.
- **Simplifications.** The replay is a throwaway projection, simpler than the real Domain; the plan asks to re-run the key scenarios on the real Domain at step 2a. Kill-test writes carry 64-byte payloads. Power loss and OS crashes are not testable.
- **Bucket list.** Q1 used the provisional list; repeat it once the list is fixed (plan risk row).

## 5. Conflicts with the plan and the ADRs

- **Dataset size.** The plan estimates 30 to 60 MB; the dataset has 169 MB, mainly because of the provisional bucket list. Not changed in the plan.
- **Event mix.** Corrected to the current format (ADR-082 to ADR-085): creates 10 % instead of 12 %, for exactly 10,000 items. Questions and thresholds unchanged.
- **Engine review trigger.** Reached on the phone:
  - for PF1, with both builds in both browsers; cause: the .NET runtime start;
  - for the 10 s replay in Chrome, even with AOT; cause: per-event decryption and the IndexedDB read, which blocks address.

  The bet card in `docs/adr/bets.md` and the ADR are not changed; the owner decides.
- **No ADR changed.** No ADR and no part of `architecture.md` was changed.
