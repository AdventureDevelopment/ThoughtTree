# Spike 2 report: content blobs and replay speed

Brief and decision rule: `docs/plan/next-work.md`, item "Spike 2: content blobs and replay speed" (owner, 2026-11-27): blobs go ahead if (2) or (3) stays within about 10 to 20 % of (1) on the phone; otherwise (4) decides, as a UX question for the owner. Blob shape: ADR-091 "Content" (Proposed). Code: `spikes/spike-2/` (disposable, outside `src/` and the gate). Raw results: `docs/spikes/spike-2/data/results/`.

**Status, 2026-11-28.** Desktop measured by the agent in headless Chromium 158, AOT (the release build) and interpreted, plus CPU throttling 4x as phone fallback. Phone and tablet **not measured yet**: section 3 estimates them from spike 1; section 5 says what the owner runs. This report recommends; the owner decides.

## 1. Result against the decision rule

- **Desktop (measured, indication only):** (2) +1 %, (3) +5 % against (1), AOT; interpreted +6 % and +8 %. With 4x CPU throttling: +9 % and +13 %.
- **Phone and tablet (estimated, not measured):** (2) +1 to +9 %, (3) +5 to +13 %, AOT; about 2.4 to 3.1 s for (1) on the Fairphone 5 and 2.2 to 2.4 s on the tablet (section 3).
- **Reading:** both stay inside "about 10 to 20 %" in every configuration measured or estimated. (2) stays at or under 10 %; (3) may pass 10 % on a slow phone. So the rule is met on this evidence, and (4) is not needed as a fallback. It is still worth having, at no replay cost: the tree without text shows 10 to 15 % sooner.
- **Recommendation:** go ahead with content blobs, with layout (3), separate event and content blocks. On replay it costs only about 4 points more than (2), and it makes erasing 3 to 4 times cheaper, because event blocks never change. One phone run in Chrome confirms it (section 5). It is needed only if the owner reads the rule as 10 % strictly; Safari has no reference at all.
- **Costs that come with blobs:** stored size +30 to +33 % (blob padding: 10.7 MB of text padded to 43.8 MB, +310 %); sync batch +53 % on the wire and about 1.5× the time to save (section 4).

## 3. Estimate for the phone and tablet (not a measurement)

**Method.** The desktop series records each part of a replay separately: IndexedDB read, Web Crypto decryption, JavaScript join and copy into .NET, JSON parsing, and applying (with the join in case 3). The rest, mostly rendering, is scaled like applying. Each part is scaled by its own spike 1 ratio, device against desktop Chromium, per browser and build (spike 1 `tables.md` T2: Fairphone 5 Chrome and Firefox, Galaxy Tab S6 Lite Chrome). Read and decrypt are estimated twice, giving the low and the high end: by the per-event ratio of T2, and by spike 1's block measurement on the phone (T10, blocks of 1,000), scaled by the bytes. The tablet has no T10 numbers, so its T10 value is the phone's, scaled by the tablet-to-phone ratio of T2. Generated by `node spikes/spike-2/harness/estimate.mjs`.

**Check of the method, and why the range in section 1 is wider.** Applied to the 4x throttled desktop (last row), the method gets the absolute time about right (5.1 to 5.3 s estimated, 4.87 s measured). It underestimates the gap between the layouts, though: +1 % and +6 % estimated against +9 % and +13 % measured. Fixed ratios per part assume that every case scales alike. Under a slower CPU, the extra work of (2) and (3) grows faster than the base: longer lines with a nested content object to parse, and the join. Spike 1 placed the phone between the desktop and 4x for .NET work. So section 1 gives the phone a range: from the part-wise estimate (+1 %, +5 %) to the 4x measurement (+9 %, +13 %).

**Where the estimate is weakest.**
- **Read and decrypt of blocks:** spike 1 measured them per block only on the phone (T10), not on the desktop or the tablet. The tablet's value is derived from per-event ratios. For replay this matters little: on the desktop, read and decrypt take about 16 % of the time, and the cases differ little in them.
- **Parse and apply** carry the gap between the cases, and spike 1 measured them only as totals per replay. Their ratio per case is an assumption, and the 4x check shows that it underestimates.
- **Writes** (erase, sync) have no spike 1 reference; they are scaled like reads.
- **Safari (iPhone, iPad):** no reference at all; no estimate is possible.
- **Throttling:** spike 1 found the 4x fallback off by about a factor of two; here it serves only as the pessimistic end.
- **The same devices only:** the Fairphone 5 has 8 GB, above the plan's 4 to 6 GB class.

**Device runs still needed.** None to decide (2), which stays near 10 % even at the pessimistic end. For (3), only if the owner reads the rule as 10 % strictly: then one run on the Fairphone 5 in Chrome settles it. That phone has the slowest parse and apply in spike 1, so it is the worst case among the reference devices. Firefox and the tablet would not change the decision. Safari needs a run on an iPhone or iPad if it is to count, because nothing estimates it.

## 4. What the numbers say per case

- **(1) to (3) replay.** Parsing JSON dominates: 747 ms of 1.12 s on the desktop with AOT. With blocks, read plus decrypt take only about 180 ms. (2) parses slightly longer lines than (1). (3) reads 7 % more block bytes and joins about 72,500 blobs by id through a dictionary: +34 ms on the desktop, +212 ms at 4x.
- **(4) structure first.** The tree without text appears 10 to 15 % sooner than in (1). The text of the first 50 visible items (85 blobs, read singly) follows in 8 to 50 ms, and all text is joined in the background about 0.25 s (desktop) to 1.2 s (4x) later. As a UX option it is cheap, but it gains little, because parsing structure dominates as well.
- **(5) erase.** A typical leaf (4 blobs in 4 blocks) takes 12 to 45 ms in (3) and 44 to 162 ms in (2). A branch of 96 items (693 blobs) touches 97 of the 100 blocks, because an item's history is spread over the whole log: 0.26 to 0.75 s in (3) against 0.67 to 2.6 s in (2). In (3), only the content blocks change and the event blocks stay. In (1), erasing is impossible without rewriting events. Every erase was checked: no erased text left in the rebuilt blocks, and no erased blob readable.
- **(6) sync batch of 1,000 events.**
  - **Bytes on the wire:** 725 blobs are added, 1.10 MB against 0.72 MB (+53 %). Almost all of it is blob padding: a title of about 60 bytes is padded to 512 bytes.
  - **Saving** (serialise, pad, encrypt, store; the acknowledgement): 150 ms against 99 ms on the desktop, and 277 against 202 ms at 4x. The block follows in 10 to 24 ms.
- **Stored size.** (1) 125 MB, (2) 163 MB (+30 %), (3) 166 MB (+33 %), counting ciphertext, nonces and ids. Without text, event envelopes shrink from 64.2 to 52.5 MB padded: most still fill the 512 B bucket. The blobs add 49 MB for 10.7 MB of text. A smaller first bucket for blobs (for example 128 or 256 B) would cut most of it. That is an ADR-087 question for the owner, not measured here.

## 5. Owner runs on the devices

Only the phone run in Chrome is needed, and only under a strict reading of the rule (section 3); Firefox, the tablet and Safari are optional. The spike runs in the device's own browser, without a computer, a cable or an account; the steps are the same in every browser.

**Getting the site.** The spike has its own `spikes/spike-2/preview-site.sh`. A pull request that changes only `spikes/spike-2/` and `docs/spikes/` therefore gets a preview site from `.github/workflows/preview-build.yml`; its address is posted on the pull request. To try it on a computer: `spikes/spike-2/harness/publish-device.sh`, then `node spikes/spike-1/harness/server.mjs --port 5280 --root spikes/spike-2/publish/device-site --headers spikes/spike-2/publish/device-site/staticwebapp.config.json`.

**Before you start.** As for spike 1:
- Battery above 50 % or charging, and power saving off.
- Screen timeout at its longest value, other apps closed.
- A normal tab, not a private one.
- About 500 MB of free storage.

**Steps:**
1. Open the preview address. It opens the spike 2 device runner.
2. Enter the device model (model only, no names), choose phone or tablet, and tap **Keep test data**.
3. Tap **Prepare test data** and leave the page in front. It generates spike 1's dataset and checks its checksum. It then stores the data three times, about 400 MB, which takes a few minutes. When it is done, the page shows "Test data ready".
4. Choose **3 rounds** (about 10 minutes; enough for the decision) or 10 rounds, and tap **Start**. The page reloads once per case and comes back by itself. If the browser closed, open the address again and tap **Continue**.
5. Tap **Share the result file** or **Save the result file**. The file is called `spike2-<phone|tablet>-<browser>-aot.json`. Attach it in the chat, or upload it to `docs/spikes/spike-2/data/results/` on the pull request's branch.
6. The agent then runs `node spikes/spike-2/harness/summarise.mjs`, which adds the device rows to section 2, and updates sections 1 and 3.

What is lost without a computer is the same as in spike 1: no forced browser restart and no CPU control. The runs are warm page loads, and only the replay part is compared, which does not depend on the app's start.

## 6. What was built, and simplifications

- **One setup page load** generates spike 1's 100,000-event dataset with spike 1's generator (by reference, unchanged) and writes three IndexedDB databases, one per layout. Each holds what that layout would store:
  - **(1)** Event envelopes with the text inside, plus local blocks of 1,000 events (ADR-086).
  - **(2)** Event envelopes without text, one blob per event that carries text (72,496 of 100,000), and blocks whose lines carry the event and its text.
  - **(3)** The same envelopes and blobs, plus event blocks without text and content blocks aligned to the same 1,000-event ranges.
- **Blobs** take their id from the event id (spike choice: the event id's second group set to `00cb`). Each has its own random nonce, is padded to the ADR-087 buckets and is encrypted with the tree key through a non-extractable handle. Its additional data is the context label, version, `KeyId`, blob id and account id. Blocks are padded to a multiple of 16 KiB, with the additional data of ADR-086.
- **Scenarios:**
  - Replays (1) to (3), each in chunks of 10 blocks, with one Web Crypto call per block in parallel.
  - (4): the structure from event blocks, the visible text from single blobs, the rest from content blocks.
  - (5): one transaction deletes the blobs, replaces the rebuilt blocks and appends the erase event. Untimed, the data is then restored for the next run.
  - (6): 1,000 envelopes, plus blobs where the layout has them, encrypted and stored in one transaction, with the block written afterwards in its own transaction. The variants are interleaved and the median of 5 rounds is taken.
- **Simplifications:**
  - The replay is spike 1's throwaway projection, not the Domain.
  - Block lines carry the event id.
  - The erase rewrites only lines whose leading id is erased.
  - Event envelopes and blobs are shared between the encryption of (2) and (3) during setup; the bytes are the same.
  - Snapshots are not used: the brief asks for a replay from blocks.
